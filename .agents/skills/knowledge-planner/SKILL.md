# Knowledge Planner

Find what is genuinely worth teaching, why it matters to the intended learner, and what understanding the eventual work must establish. This Skill is medium-independent.

## Responsibility And Handoff

Own the learning promise, audience starting point, teaching scope, core mechanisms and distinctions, necessary evidence, and the progression of understanding. Do not own final prose, narration, scene direction, visual treatment, timing, or implementation. When the medium is a Remotion video, hand off to `remotion-video-director`; otherwise hand off to the relevant medium-specific creator.

The handoff is a **teaching contract**, not a completed script or a terse outline. Specify the indispensable understanding and reasoning that must survive adaptation, while leaving editorial and visual decisions to the downstream creator. Answer targeted requests to clarify unsupported claims, missing prerequisites, or changes to the learning promise; do not take over the downstream script.

## Choose The Learning Promise

Seek a worthwhile change in the learner's mental model or capability, not exhaustive coverage. A topic category or a collection of definitions is not yet a teaching point. Identify the misconception, tension, real decision, contrast, or capability that makes the subject worth understanding. Decide what belongs outside the scope.

Do not default to an evenly weighted glossary when asked to explain a family of terms. Find the mechanisms or distinctions that unlock the rest; use a catalogue when lookup or classification is itself the promised outcome.

Keep factual claims honest. Distinguish verified facts, interpretations, illustrative hypotheticals, and simplifications. Use credible primary evidence where the topic is current or consequential; do not invent evidence to make a story work.

## Build The Understanding

**Prerequisites.** Establish what the intended learner likely knows. Identify indispensable terms and assumptions that need introduction before later reasoning depends on them. An unfamiliar term may appear in the hook as a question, but its working meaning must be established before it is used to justify a conclusion. Do not ask the learner to evaluate a hypothesis they cannot yet understand.

**Explanation.** For each central claim, identify the relevant meaning, conditions, causal or conceptual mechanism, observable consequences, and limits of the evidence. Include only what the learning promise needs, not a mandatory five-part structure. An instruction such as “check the evidence” is not a substitute for showing which evidence matters, why, and what it cannot establish. In particular, do not infer unobservable intentions from market or behavioral outcomes.

**Learning progression.** Organize these explanations around questions the learner can now ask or changes they can perceive. Where useful, let a transaction, experiment, artifact, problem, or model evolve, carrying its consequential state into later beats. A story wrapper is not a substitute for teaching; equally, do not force characters, suspense, or linear causality onto genuinely independent comparisons or proofs. Each important beat should establish understanding needed for the next or resolve a meaningful question.

**Depth and scope.** Be concise in expression, complete in indispensable reasoning. Do not compress intermediate causal steps into slogans or pad with irrelevant detail to simulate depth. If the promised breadth cannot be taught sufficiently, narrow the promise or propose a deeper treatment or series. For video, assess the explanation burden without imposing a default duration; detailed narration and timing belong to the director. If the user specified an incompatible duration, surface the trade-off rather than silently dropping reasoning.

## Evidence And Demonstration

Select examples, comparisons, artifacts, or demos only when they expose a mechanism, reveal a contrast, test an assumption, or substantiate a claim. For a demonstration, identify its meaningful input or action, visible result, and inference. State simulation assumptions and omitted complexity. Prefer a concrete change over static terminology when teaching how something works.

## Deliverable And Acceptance

Give the downstream creator enough substance to preserve the teaching: learning promise and intended learner; scope and indispensable prerequisites; the central question or evolving situation; necessary reasoning bridges and their evidence or demonstration; meaningful limits and uncertainty; and what understanding must remain after adaptation. Use natural organization and proportional detail, not a fixed output form. Differentiate indispensable reasoning from optional enrichment. A compact brief means no waste, not missing explanations.

Before handoff, test the plan against a fresh situation: could the learner explain *why* the essential result follows, distinguish it from a plausible alternative, or apply it appropriately? Could a polished set of labels and slogans satisfy the brief without teaching the mechanism? Can each prerequisite be understood before it is used? If the proposed progression can be reordered arbitrarily, check whether it is merely a disguised glossary—but do not invent causality where none exists. Repair any failing teaching requirement before handing off.
